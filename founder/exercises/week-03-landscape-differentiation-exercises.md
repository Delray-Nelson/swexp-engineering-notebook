# Week 3 Exercises: Landscape, Review Mining & Copyability (JBF Dating)

## Exercise 1 — Steelman the Incumbent

### Incumbent Founder Pitch (Why Customers Should Ignore JBF Dating)
"Look—dating only works if there are actually real, active people near you who want to meet up, and we already have the local liquidity and established product familiarity in your city that a brand-new humor app simply doesn't have yet. You don't need another experimental niche app with a thin local pool when you can open an app where people are already matching, chatting, and going on dates tonight, with structured prompts and familiar safety/trust norms already built in. Trying a tiny launch app first means risking empty queues and unvetted early dynamics just to chase a novelty hook."

### Evidence-Backed Two-Sentence Response (No Aspiration)
Right now, an incumbent genuinely beats a single-metro JBF MVP on local match density and established brand familiarity, which is why we are scoping our initial test to a concentrated local pool and measuring whether humor-first prompts actually produce faster, higher-quality replies than generic openers. We are not claiming we already have nationwide liquidity—our current evidence focuses on testing whether banter-led matching solves small-talk fatigue well enough to earn repeat engagement where local activity exists (`assumption, confidence: medium`).

## Exercise 2 — Review Mining

### Complaint Themes Extracted From Public App Reviews & Community Threads
1. **Repetitive "Hey / How's Your Week" Dead Ends (Hinge & Bumble):** Users repeatedly complain that matches stall after low-effort greetings or generic prompt replies that never turn into real chemistry (`source: app store reviews & community threads`). *(Maps to canvas pain: small-talk exhaustion.)*
2. **Swipe Burnout & Photo-First Superficiality (Tinder & Bumble):** Reviewers describe exhausting endless swiping where appearance drives matches but conversations feel shallow, transactional, or one-sided (`source: app store reviews & community threads`). *(Maps to canvas pain: superficial photo bias & low-signal matching.)*
3. **Ghosting & Sudden Conversational Drop-Off (Hinge, Bumble & Tinder):** Users frequently note matches disappearing mid-chat or never replying after the first exchange, wasting time invested in crafting messages (`source: app store reviews & community threads`). *(Maps to canvas pain: wasted time on low-signal interactions.)*
4. **Paywall / Subscription Frustration Without Better Outcomes (Hinge, Bumble & Tinder):** Reviewers complain about aggressive upsells, boosts, and monthly paywalls that feel expensive relative to actual date quality (`source: app store reviews & community threads; public pricing`). *(Maps to canvas pain: high cost / low perceived return.)*
5. **Newly Surfaced Pain — "Performance Fatigue" & Awkward Tone Mismatch (Hinge & Meme/Niche Apps):** Reviewers note that clever prompts or quirky memes can feel performative or miss the mark when one person treats it like a stand-up audition while the other wants casual, natural banter (`source: app store reviews & community threads; assumption, confidence: medium`). *(Added to `founder/value-prop-canvas.md` customer side.)*

## Exercise 3 — The Copy Drill (One-Year Quarter-by-Quarter Clone Scenario)

Assume JBF Dating's sequential humor/lore banter flow gets traction in a launch metro and a well-funded incumbent tries to clone it over four quarters:

- **Q1 (Fast Feature Clone):** The rival ships a "Humor / Banter Prompts" badge, joke-opener templates, and a meme filter in a single release cycle using their existing engineering team and massive user base. *What they copy fast:* surface UI prompts, templates, and superficial humor tags. *Trade-off avoided so far:* low effort for them, but it sits bolted onto a high-volume swipe/small-talk funnel.
- **Q2 (Price & Visibility Push):** The rival bundles humor filters into an existing subscription tier or discounts access while pushing notifications to their large local user base. *What they copy fast:* pricing/bundling mechanics and distribution reach. *Friction emerging:* forcing humor-first depth into a mainstream feed risks confusing users who open the app for fast swipes or serious/polite dating.
- **Q3 (Core Funnel Tension):** To make sequential banter and lore callbacks work rather than feel like a gimmick, the rival has to change matching and conversation flow—yet doing so slows down their high-volume swipe/like velocity and risks alienating core users who prefer conventional profiles (`source: founder/competitor-matrix.md; assumption, confidence: low`).
- **Q4 (Retention & Signal Divergence):** Even with more money and distribution, the rival faces a product identity split between mass-market volume/monetization and low-pressure, authentic two-way banter without performance fatigue. Meanwhile, JBF's durability depends on whether multi-turn humor/lore interaction creates genuine compatibility signal and tight local community fit that a bolted-on feature lacks.

### Copyability Rung Check (Confirmed vs. Corrected)
- **Confirmed rung:** **Segment focus** (with an early hypothesis toward **structural/data insight** only if sequential banter proves predictive of real compatibility). A single prompt or lower price alone is easily copied or undercut; JBF's real edge is building the entire flow, language, and local launch around banter-first matching while incumbents hesitate to fragment their mass-market funnel.
