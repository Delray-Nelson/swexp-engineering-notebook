# Competitor Landscape & Matrix: JBF Dating (Just Be Funny)

## Part 1 — Census (6 Alternatives Across Three Rings)

### Ring 1 — Direct Competitors (Humor-Forward & Prompt-Led Dating Apps)
1. **Hinge** — Prompt-and-comment dating app positioned around "designed to be deleted," using written/voice prompts to spark conversation (`source: Hinge positioning & user complaints`).
2. **Bumble** — Mainstream dating app featuring structured Opening Moves and time-bound connection mechanics to reduce blank-box stalls (`source: Bumble product positioning & user complaints`).
3. **Schmooze (Humor/Meme-Based Dating Niche)** — Niche meme-swiping dating app that matches users on humor preferences rather than standard photo bios (`source: app store & community threads`).

### Ring 2 — Adjacent Substitutes (High-Volume Swipe & Interest Platforms)
4. **Tinder** — High-volume, photo-first swipe app dominant for local density and fast browsing, but widely associated with generic openers and swipe burnout (`source: user complaints & market reviews`).
5. **Coffee Meets Bagel / Filter-Heavy Apps** — Curated daily batch / preference-filtered dating apps prioritizing selectivity and lower daily volume over rapid banter (`source: app reviews & community threads`).

### Ring 3 — Status Quo & DIY Workarounds
6. **Status Quo (No App / Casual IRL / Off-App Social Circles & Events)** — Meeting people organically through friends, comedy/social nights, hobbies, or taking a break from dating apps altogether (`source: discovery interviews & value-prop canvas`).
   - *Properties:* $0 subscription fee; spontaneous real-world tone and chemistry; high time, luck, and social-energy friction to consistently meet single, humor-compatible people.

## Part 2 — Decision Dimensions & Matrix

### Selected Decision Dimensions (Evidence-Justified)
1. **Humor & Lore Banter Fit:** Derived from discovery/canvas evidence that singles lose motivation to dry small talk and want shared wit, humor style, and cultural/lore fluency early on (`source: founder/value-prop-canvas.md`).
2. **Time-to-First-Spark:** Derived from user complaints that matching on photos still leaves users stuck in "hey / how's your week" dead ends before any real chemistry happens (`source: discovery & 1-star review themes`).
3. **Total Cost (Money + Hidden Time):** Derived from user frustration over spending hours swiping low-signal profiles and paying ~$22–$33/mo for boosted visibility without better conversations (`source: Match Group / Bumble pricing & discovery notes`).
4. **Local Liquidity & Match Density:** Derived from market-sizing sensitivity analysis showing that a dating app fails if users cannot find active, nearby people in their own metro (`source: founder/market-sizing.md`).
5. **Low-Friction Psychological Comfort:** Derived from users abandoning apps that feel performative, judgmental, or exhausting to maintain (`source: founder/value-prop-canvas.md`).

## Part 3 — The Map and the Gap

### Two Heaviest Dimensions
1. **X-Axis — Humor & Lore Banter Fit** (Low = photo/swipe or generic small talk $\rightarrow$ High = sequential humor prompts, lore fluency & playful back-and-forth)
2. **Y-Axis — Local Liquidity & Match Density** (Low = sparse/niche local pool $\rightarrow$ High = dense, active local dating pool)

### Competitor Placement & Empty Region
- **Top-Left (High Local Liquidity, Low/Med Banter Fit):** *Tinder, Bumble, Hinge* — deep local pools in major metros, but conversations routinely stall in generic openers or safe Q&A small talk.
- **Bottom-Left (Low Local Liquidity, Low/Med Banter Fit):** *Smaller generalist/filter apps* — lack both metro scale and distinctive banter mechanics.
- **Bottom-Right (High Banter Fit, Low Local Liquidity):** *Schmooze* and *JBF Dating (Near-Term MVP)* — strong humor-first hook, constrained initially by local density.
- **Empty Region — Top-Right (High Local Liquidity + High Humor & Lore Banter Fit):** No incumbent combines dense metro liquidity with deep, multi-turn humor/lore banter that bypasses dry small talk.

### Opening or Graveyard? (Three Sentences)
The **Top-Right quadrant** is a genuine **opening** for a single-metro beachhead rather than a graveyard, provided JBF concentrates liquidity in one city/scene at a time instead of spreading thin nationwide. Discovery notes and recurring one-star reviews across mainstream dating apps consistently cite small-talk exhaustion, dead-end "hey" openers, and shallow photo swiping as top reasons singles disengage (`source: founder/value-prop-canvas.md & app review themes`). However, whether humor-first banter converts stated fatigue into sustained retention and local meetups remains an active Week 4 behavioral test rather than a solved assumption (`assumption, confidence: medium`).

## Part 4 — Differentiation Claim and Intelligence

### Differentiation claim
We win **small-talk-fatigued singles in our launch metro who connect through wit, shared humor style, and cultural lore** because **JBF replaces blank-box small talk and static photo swiping with a progressive humor-and-banter sequence that reveals real conversational spark before a match stalls**.
- **True because:** Our core product flow is built around multi-turn humor prompts, lore callbacks, and playful banter mechanics rather than a photo-first swipe deck (`source: founder/value-prop-canvas.md & founder/research/humor-belief-alignment-test.md`).
- **Valued because it maps to:** Eliminating small-talk exhaustion, awkward blank-message openers, and wasted hours on low-signal matches while delivering immediate playful chemistry (`source: founder/value-prop-canvas.md`).
- **Copyability rung:** **Segment focus** (built end-to-end for humor-first, lore-fluent daters in a concentrated metro launch, with an ambition to build proprietary sequential banter/compatibility signals over time).
- **What stops a richer competitor copying this in a quarter:** Large incumbents optimize for mass-market volume, photo-driven swipe velocity, and broad mainstream appeal; re-architecting their core matching and onboarding around multi-turn humor/lore filtering would add friction to their high-volume funnel and alienate casual swipers.

### Intelligence takeaways
- **Price anchors observed:** Mainstream dating apps charge **~$22–$33/month** (`~$264–$396/year`) for core paid tiers, establishing clear consumer willingness to pay for better dating outcomes and validating JBF's **$10–$15/month** (`$120–$180/year`) challenger price anchor (`source: founder/market-sizing.md`).
- **Crowded positions to avoid:** High-volume photo swiping ("most matches fast"), generic "serious/intentional relationship" badges, and pay-to-be-seen boost mechanics—all are saturated by Tinder, Hinge, and Bumble.
- **Unserved pains from competitor reviews:** Repetitive "hey / how was your day" openers that die within three messages, feeling judged solely on photos before personality comes across, and paying monthly subscriptions while still matching with unresponsive or incompatible people.
- **Concrete next decision fed by intelligence:** Keep JBF's MVP scope tightly focused on **1) a 5–7 prompt sequential banter/reaction onboarding & matching test in one metro** and **2) testing a $10–$15/month challenger tier** rather than building expensive video, event, or broad multi-city features first.
