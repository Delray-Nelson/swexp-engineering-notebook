# TAM, SAM, SOM Analysis: JBF Dating (Just Be Funny)

## 1. The Three Circles, Defined by the Decision They Inform

### TAM — Total Addressable Market
The total annual revenue available if every person or company that has the problem bought a solution at your price. TAM answers: is this problem economically significant at all? It deliberately ignores competition, reach, and your own limits.

### SAM — Serviceable Addressable Market
The slice of TAM your actual product, in its actual form, could serve: filtered by your segment, geography, language, platform, and product scope as it realistically exists over the next couple of years. SAM answers: how big is the pond you are actually fishing in?

### SOM — Serviceable Obtainable Market
The slice of SAM you can realistically win in a defined near-term window, given real competition, your budget, and your distribution ability — which for you, right now, is one technical founder in a program, shipping between lessons. SOM answers: what is the honest near-term prize, and is it worth the effort? SOM is the only number of the three you can be held accountable to.

The circles nest: SOM inside SAM inside TAM. Each is a different tool. TAM sanity-checks the ambition; SAM defines the battlefield; SOM sets the plan. Investors, when you get to the funding weeks, mostly use TAM to check ceiling and SOM to check judgment — a wildly padded SOM is a judgment failure, not an ambition signal.

## 2. Tagged Inputs (Sources & Confidence Levels)

- **U.S. adults who have ever used a dating site or app:** ~30% of U.S. adults overall (~52% of never-married U.S. adults) `(source: Pew Research Center, 2023)`
- **U.S. adults aged 18–29 online dating usage benchmark:** ~53% `(source: Pew Research Center / dating market benchmarks)`
- **U.S. online dating market revenue cross-check:** ~$3.23B–$3.47B/year (2025–2026) `(source: Market Data Forecast; North America ~83.1% U.S. share via Mordor Intelligence)`
- **Paying user monthly ARPU on major apps:** ~$21.96–$33.11/month on Hinge and Bumble tiers `(source: Match Group / Bumble public filings)`
- **Dating app burnout / fatigue signal:** Up to ~78% of surveyed users report burnout `(source: Forbes Health survey benchmark)`
- **Baseline U.S. addressable dating-app user pool:** ~35,000,000 U.S. dating app users `(assumption, confidence: medium)`
- **JBF challenger annual price per paying user:** $120 – $180 / year (~$10–$15/mo challenger tier) `(assumption, confidence: medium)`
- **Segment fraction experiencing small-talk / app fatigue:** 50% – 70% `(assumption, confidence: medium)`
- **Geography / language / platform filter (initial metro launch):** 1.5% – 3.0% (single major metro + English mobile web/app) `(assumption, confidence: high)`
- **Near-term product scope filter (humor-first banter MVP):** 55% – 65% `(assumption, confidence: medium)`
- **12–24 month reachable user pool (solo technical founder channels):** 15,000 – 40,000 users `(assumption, confidence: low)`
- **Paid conversion rate on reachable users:** 3% – 6% `(assumption, confidence: medium)`

## 3. Bottom-Up Equations for JBF Dating

### TAM
`TAM = (number of entities with the problem) x (annual revenue per entity)`
- Low: `35,000,000 users x $120/yr` = **~$4.2B / year**
- High: `35,000,000 users x $180/yr` = **~$6.3B / year**
- *Cross-check:* Aligns within the same order of magnitude as the ~$3.2B–$3.5B U.S. online dating revenue market.

### SAM (2-Year Metro-Led Scope)
`SAM = TAM x (segment fraction) x (geo/language/platform fraction) x (near-term product scope fraction)`
- Low: `$4.2B x 0.50 x 0.015 x 0.55` = **~$17.3M / year**
- High: `$6.3B x 0.70 x 0.030 x 0.65` = **~$86.0M / year**

### SOM (Next 12–24 Months, Solo Founder Distribution)
`SOM = (reachable customers via operated channels) x (defensible conversion fraction) x (annual revenue per customer)`
- Low: `15,000 reached x 3% conversion (450 payers) x $120/yr` = **~$54,000 / year**
- High: `40,000 reached x 6% conversion (2,400 payers) x $180/yr` = **~$432,000 / year**

## 4. Defensibility Verdict & Next Verification Step

1. **Problem & Ceiling Are Real:** High user burnout on swipe/small-talk apps and multi-billion-dollar category spend confirm the problem is real and economically meaningful.
2. **Liquidity & Distribution Are the Binding Constraints:** Because dating apps require local density rather than scattered signups, SOM is tightly bounded by how many users you can concentrate in one launch city and convert past early churn.
3. **Highest-Leverage Check Next:** Test real user behavior around banter-first prompts and local density (whether users actually complete humor prompts and return) before scaling engineering scope.
